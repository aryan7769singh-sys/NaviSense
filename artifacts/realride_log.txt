READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING ANY NUMBER ABOVE

1. VelNet beats predict-the-mean on 1 of 5 rides (214016 only).
   It is NOT a working speed estimator on this hardware or these vehicles.

2. Failure DIRECTION tracks vibration (r=+0.67, n=5):
     auto  hf 0.398 -> over-predicts by +4.22 m/s
     metro hf 0.052 -> predicts 0.7 m/s while actually doing 13.4 m/s
   The model maps road roughness to speed. A rail vehicle has none, so it
   reads as stationary. This is the model doing what it was trained to do,
   off-distribution - not a bug.

3. Failure MAGNITUDE tracks alignment (r=+0.61 with skill, n=5).
   BUT alignment CONVERGED on 3 rides (0.564/0.732/0.771) and VelNet still
   failed on 2 of those 3. Fixing attitude alone will NOT fix the speed model.
   This contradicts the 'attitude is the bottleneck' gate in the current plan.

4. Do NOT quote 'ride length vs skill r=-0.92'. It is a confound: the
   predict-the-mean baseline is strong on constant-speed rides, so the long
   metro ride has a low baseline (2.98) that is hard to beat. Not a finding.

5. Strapdown at 10 s is 9.8-16.7% on the four ROAD rides - at or near the
   10% spec - and diverges to 23-102% by 60 s. Adaptive weighting by blackout
   duration is the obvious unexploited win. It is not implemented.

6. n = 21-100 blackouts per ride, ONE device, ONE day, one auto and one metro.
   Directional evidence. Not a result.


================================================================================
FILTER EXPERIMENTS ON THE SAME 5 RIDES   (exp_qv_real.py, exp_ramp_real.py)
================================================================================

  configuration          10s drift pass | 30s drift pass | 60s drift pass
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  current (sigma as-is)     45.6    6%  |   49.3    4%   |   52.5    5%
  flat sigma x100           27.5   24%  |   42.8   11%   |   54.7    6%
  ramp k=100 tau=10s        28.3   24%  |   44.0    8%   |   47.7    5%
  ramp k=300 tau=20s        27.4   24%  |   42.6   13%   |   52.8    5%
  STRAPDOWN (no model)      25.2   30%  |   38.9   15%   |   55.2    6%

1. q_v (process noise) is IRRELEVANT. Swept 0.6 -> 0.002, a 300x range;
   drift moves under 1.5 points. You cannot fix a bad R by tuning Q.

2. VelNet is OVERCONFIDENT BY ~100x on these vehicles. Inflating its reported
   sigma x100 cuts 10 s drift 45.6 -> 27.5 and quadruples pass rate 6% -> 24%.
   The NLL uncertainty head was calibrated on IO-VNBD cars and does not
   transfer. This is why the filter could not down-weight a model that was
   15 km/h wrong: it was told the model was certain.

3. CROSSOVER CONFIRMED near 60 s, from both directions. Inflating sigma helps
   at 10-30 s and HURTS at 60 s (52.5 -> 54.7). Integration is better early,
   the model is better late.

4. THE UNCOMFORTABLE RESULT. Strapdown with NO MODEL AT ALL still wins at
   10 s (25.2% / 30% pass) and 30 s (38.9% / 15% pass) against every filter
   configuration tried. The model only earns its place at 60 s, where the
   ramp gives 47.7% against strapdown's 55.2%.
   Short blackouts - tunnels, flyovers, the PRIMARY use case - are currently
   best served by deleting the neural network.

5. Even with the model fully suppressed (sigma x1000) fused gives 27.7% while
   strapdown gives 25.2%. The residual gap is the ZUPT/bias machinery, not
   the model. Worth checking whether the stationary detector false-fires.

6. k and tau were chosen ON THESE SAME RIDES. Fitting on the evaluation set.
   Upper bound on headroom, NOT a validated result. Needs a held-out drive.

WHERE IS THE SPEED CUE ACTUALLY?   (first real test of the 400 Hz thesis)
================================================================================

Correlation |r| between log band-energy of accel magnitude and GNSS speed,
4 s windows, moving samples only:

  ride       0.5-2Hz  2-5Hz  5-15Hz 15-40Hz 40-90Hz 90-180Hz
  193941      0.48    0.35    0.42    0.14    0.11    0.00   metro
  201334      0.27    0.11    0.02    0.02    0.02    0.02
  212922      0.43    0.05    0.29    0.20    0.17    0.35
  214016      0.80    0.15    0.73    0.43    0.28    0.15   <- VelNet works here
  221327      0.05    0.27    0.12    0.52    0.30    0.15   auto
  MEAN        0.40    0.19    0.32    0.27    0.18    0.13

  below 5 Hz (IO-VNBD can see this):  mean |r| = 0.30
  above 5 Hz (400 Hz was to unlock):  mean |r| = 0.22

THE 400 Hz THESIS IS NOT SUPPORTED ON AVERAGE. The strongest band is the
LOWEST one, 0.5-2 Hz - suspension resonance, which 10 Hz data captures fine.
We have argued for weeks that the cue lives above 5 Hz and that IO-VNBD's
10 Hz destroyed it. On the first data able to test that, it does not hold.

TWO REAL QUALIFICATIONS, both important:
  - The AUTO is the exception and a strong one: 0.05 at 0.5-2 Hz but 0.52 at
    15-40 Hz. For three-wheelers the cue genuinely IS high-frequency, and
    high-rate capture genuinely does pay. The thesis is right for that class.
  - 214016, the only ride where VelNet works, has by far the strongest cue in
    any band (0.80 / 0.73). Cue strength predicts model success. Consistent.

DO NOT OVERREAD: this is a LINEAR correlation of a scalar band energy. VelNet
learns a nonlinear function of 8 channels over 10 s. Weak linear correlation
is not proof the information is absent. But mean |r| ~0.3 in the best band,
across 5 rides, says the signal is weak in this data - not that the model
merely needs more capacity.

ALSO MEASURED: vibration->speed slope is unstable WITHIN a ride (551% drift
between first and second half) and varies in sign between rides. A hand-fitted
per-vehicle scale factor will not work. R2 is ~0.00 on 4 of 5 rides.
